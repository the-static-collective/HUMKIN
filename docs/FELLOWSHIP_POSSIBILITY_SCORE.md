## Revised Dec 4 role-appropriate integration

Rather than running a separate tiny "new social experiment" in a venue where the creator already helps host and perform, design **one opt-in musical act within the actual run of show**, conditional on Paula and church-side production approval:

1. **Before the room:** ask Paula and the show-caller where a 20–40-second spoken transition fits around the requested guitar set or duet, not during prayer, free supper, or another performer's slot.
2. **Onstage:** let a performed or played HUMKIN song carry the creative moment. A single clear line offers an invitation that can continue *after* the set: "If there's a small thing you could make easier for someone here, or a thing we could make together, there's room for that conversation tonight."
3. **Offstage:** a separate optional, staffed table already compatible with the existing OFFER / NEED / BUILD / QUESTION host kit receives only consented small opportunities; no sign-up or hardship disclosure is needed to enjoy the music.
4. **After closing:** with permission, document exactly one independently confirmed introduction, fulfilled act, future collaborator commitment, or second-host invitation. Report failures too. Avoid collecting private stories publicly or claiming completed help from a promise.
5. **Across the biweekly cycle:** design a tiny reprise / handoff to the next creative host. A recurring musical refrain may symbolically connect the nights, but no other host is obligated to repeat the experiment.

**Innovation bar:** The event is already a *live venue, participatory open mic, hospitality table and repeatable host cycle*. The novel question is whether the music/performance can create **a measurable additional useful collaboration** without becoming a required activity, marketing funnel or claim of church endorsement. No new software is required to test that.

## The central invention hypothesis

> A song is not merely a thing to hear, or a link to propagate. A song can be an **address for an unfinished good**: a small action, a specific need, a spare resource, or a next question. A successful transmission does not end with a scan. It ends with a new, voluntary capacity in the room.

HUMKIN is the invitation; Fellowship is the human context; MOTIF is a future question-and-possibility engine; reLATTE can eventually route consented useful work or exchange. **No new software or participation requirement is necessary for the first proof.**

This is an original proposed combination, **not** a claim that gift music, community matchmaking, time banking, or interactive performances were invented here.

## Six deliberately distinct branches

### A. The Song With Unfinished Business (near-term)
A person encounters a HUMKIN song, then optionally offers one tiny **CAN**, **NEED**, or **MAKE**. A host looks for a consented, real-world match. The outcome (not the person's identity) is the "second verse." Example: someone has spare craft material; another can use it; a match happens before the event ends. A song is always free even if no one volunteers anything.

### B. The Counterfactual Jukebox (MOTIF frontier)
Instead of asking what track someone likes, ask which *next action* would make the most difference in the immediate situation. Options like "find a chair for someone," "find a collaborator," or "bring a needed supply" are offered only if actually relevant. Optimize for useful matches, not plays, engagement or survey responses. Human moderation first; reasoning algorithms after there is grounded evidence.

### C. The Unfinished Chorus (participatory score)
The room writes a procedural composition out of small acts: someone names a question, another contributes an answer, a third contributes a way to do it. The completed act is a musical "cadence" even if no audio is played. With explicit permissions, the next HUMKIN song or postcard may be associated with an anonymized, verified story of what became possible.

### D. The Human Repeater (forkable fellowship)
Give one willing person a tiny reproducible protocol rather than mere promo material:
1) play or send a song if wanted;
2) ask a nonintrusive question that could lead to help;
3) connect one consenting person or opportunity;
4) pass along the protocol.
It should work in a gym, kitchen, porch, farm, or gathering without the original operator or institutional backend. Success is **independent useful adoption**, not number of copies printed.

### E. The Missing Third Person (relationship geometry)
Most exchanges are imagined as A gives to B. Many valuable acts require a third node: A has something; B can transport or adapt it; C can use it. Model *capabilities*, not merely commodities. At the event, draw three anonymous circles on paper and identify a real three-step match. Never solicit or publicly expose vulnerable people's contact information.

### F. The Future Echo (time delay)
A person leaves an unfinished but safe, voluntary promise—"I can teach a chord," "I can share recipe," "I can lend a folding table"—for the *next* gathering. The song returns when the action happens; the relay becomes durable community memory. Collect only what is necessary with informed consent; anonymous public reporting should not imply a promise was fulfilled without evidence.

## December 4 portable field protocol

### Level 0: fallback when only private conversation is appropriate
Carry a pen, a few small cards, and a phone with the existing HUMKIN surprise song. Talk privately with willing adults; don't place posters or put out cards without permission.

Conversation opening:
> "I'm trying a strange music experiment. The song is yours to hear or ignore. Is there one tiny thing that would become easier tonight if two people happened to meet?"

If the person isn't interested, end the exchange pleasantly. No demand to listen, volunteer, or reveal a need. If a match is possible, ask consent separately before introducing anyone. Privately note **only** whether a genuine introduction or act happened; don't record names or sensitive needs.

### Level 1: one approved table or counter
Three plainly labeled slips or bowls: **I CAN**, **I COULD USE**, **WE COULD MAKE**. Small practical exchanges only; no money obligations, no emergency services, no identifying stories exposed publicly. A human steward reviews and matches offers. Visitors may choose a HUMKIN link or card with no further interaction. At most one clear sign: **A SONG IS FREE. A LITTLE HELP IS OPTIONAL.**

### Level 2: integrated host/performer set, subject to final production approval
A 30–60 second invitation, not a takeover:
> "HUMKIN is free music. Tonight we're trying one more thing: whether a song can help two people make something useful happen. If you have something small to share or a little project someone could help with, find us after this. Nothing to sign up for."

No public call to disclose personal hardship. No assumed sound system, projected slides, or performance rights. Use any performance only with organizers' express permission and the necessary rights.

## Observation and decision record

After the evening, note:
- **Permission level:** visitor / table / invited stage.
- **Actual invitations attempted:** number of consensual conversations.
- **Potential edges:** how many voluntary, actionable opportunities emerged.
- **Introductions:** how many were accepted by both parties.
- **Verified outcomes:** something concretely became easier, changed custody, or was completed.
- **Independent forks:** another willing person adopted the method without the original operator asking them to promote HUMKIN.
- **Failures:** confusion, coercion, nonmatches, safety issues, unwanted commercial promotion, or needless friction.

Do not record children's information, private needs or identifying details. Do not publish venue/person names without specific approval. Anecdotal counts are observations, not statistically rigorous proof.

**Disconfirmation test:** If QR music alone is appreciated but action matches almost never happen, the "song as executable social protocol" mechanism did **not** work in that setting. Keep the music gift and change the social protocol rather than inventing impact.
